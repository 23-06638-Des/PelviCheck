Landmark Quick Guide

1. PSIS (Posterior Superior Iliac Spine) The small bony protrusion or dimple on your upper posterior hip / lower back side.
2. ASIS (Anterior Superior Iliac Spine) The front palpable bony point at the top front of your hip pelvis.

Neutral Pelvis

A balanced position where the pelvis is evenly aligned without excessive tilting forward or backward.

  • Angle range: ~7° to 15° tilt down from PSIS to ASIS.
  • Optimal load distribution on lumbar spine.
  • Balanced engagement of abdominal and hip flexor muscle groups.

Anterior Pelvic Tilt (APT)

Occurs when the front of the pelvis drops down relative to the back, deepening the lower back curve (hyperlordosis).

  • Angle range: > 15°.
  • Common among sedentary desk workers and prolonged sitting.
  • Often associated with tight hip flexors and weak gluteal/core muscles.

Posterior Pelvic Tilt (PPT)

Occurs when the pelvis rotates backward, tucking the tailbone under and flattening the natural arch of the lumbar spine.

  • Angle range: < 7°.
  • Can lead to slouching posture and increased pressure on spinal discs.
  • Often associated with tight hamstrings and weak lower back extensors.

What Does the Research Say?

It is crucial to note that variation in pelvic tilt is extremely common and normal in healthy, asymptomatic populations. According to published biomechanical studies:

85% / 75% Anterior Pelvic Tilt Prevalence Observed in asymptomatic males / females
9% / 18% Neutral Pelvic Alignment Observed in asymptomatic males / females
6% / 7% Posterior Pelvic Tilt Observed in asymptomatic males / females

Reference: Herrington, L. (2011). Assessment of the degree of pelvic tilt within a normal asymptomatic population. Manual Therapy, 16(6), 620-623.

Medical Disclaimer & Consent

Please read carefully before using PelviCheck

1. Preliminary Screening Aid: PelviCheck provides estimated 2D pelvic tilt measurements based on photographic landmarks. It is intended solely as an educational and posture awareness screening tool.

2. Not a Clinical Diagnosis: This tool DOES NOT provide clinical diagnosis, medical evaluation, or treatment plans. Photographic screening cannot account for 3D anatomical structures, soft tissue variation, or spinal pathologies.

3. Normal Biological Variation: Asymmetrical pelvic tilt is a common anatomical variation present in both symptomatic and asymptomatic individuals.

4. Professional Care: If you experience persistent lower back pain, hip discomfort, or functional limitations, consult a licensed healthcare professional or physical therapist directly.

Landmark Placement Guide

For accurate measurements:

  1. Stand in a natural, comfortable posture facing strictly to the side (90° side profile).
  2. Palpate your lower back dimple/hip bone to locate the PSIS (Back Point). Click first.
  3. Locate the prominent bone tip on the front top of your hip to place the ASIS (Front Point). Click second.
  4. Ensure lighting is clear and fitted clothing is worn for precise anatomical landmark identification.
